# Day-one delivery contract

**Current status: NOT READY.** This contract records the operator's agreed requirements. The implementation checkpoint `57560aa` has useful local and browser evidence, but the complete workflow has not passed. It is a development checkpoint, not day-one sign-off.

This contract and the [content contract](content-contract.md) apply to every contributor, including Codex and Claude. Current capabilities, implementation limits and historical test reports describe progress; they do not reduce these requirements. Only an explicit operator instruction can change the agreed delivery scope. Routine implementation choices need no additional permission.

## Required outcome

An operator can present an authorized application through local HTTPS to a browser or scanner, mask its identity and ordinary display content, and retain the application's usable technical surface and observable security behaviour. Direct routing and Tor are independent of content masking. The required Tor workflow includes an onion target, human CAPTCHA completion, and successful continuation of the original application session.

Acceptance must establish both privacy and fidelity. A page that loads after losing its controls, a perfectly sized response that loses a finding, or an isolated browser that can no longer use the application does not meet the requirement. Diagnostic preservation must also mask configured domain/product identities: a blanket diagnostic exemption that exposes them is not a solution. Provenance remains explicit; neutral content is not a promise of undetectability or of bypassing another system's safeguards.

The required media, encodings, streaming behaviour and browser APIs follow from the application workflows above. A GIF substituted for a functional document/font, a synthetic 502 replacing a required encoding, or buffered output breaking a required streaming interaction remains a fidelity gap. Contributors may not remove a failing workflow or reclassify a required behaviour as optional to obtain a pass. Where two requirements conflict or a finite representation makes a requested guarantee impossible, explain the concrete conflict and bring it to the operator; do not silently weaken either requirement or claim it is solved.

## Sign-off rules

Before closing a requirement, record the named acceptance matrix: applications/workflows, built-in and custom CAPTCHA providers, browsers/scanners and versions, macOS/Ubuntu environments, routing modes, and the resources/protocols those workflows use. Unresolved selections remain visible as NOT RUN. Choosing only easy cases cannot exclude a known required workflow or turn absent provider coverage into universal support. Contributors may add coverage freely; removing or narrowing agreed cases requires an explicit operator scope decision.

1. The delivery matrix uses **PASS**, **FAIL** and **NOT RUN**. A skip, timeout, unavailable client/provider/network or unexecuted branch is NOT RUN, never PASS. Observed incorrect behaviour remains FAIL.
2. Each required row needs repeatable evidence for the release candidate and its actual serving build. Keep synthetic, browser, scanner, live-provider and live-Tor results separate. A passing subset cannot close the whole row.
3. Acceptance has to exercise the integrated workflow. Separate component successes from different revisions cannot be assembled into an unrun end-to-end success. Preserve historical evidence and rerun affected cases after changes; record the coverage and revision of retained evidence explicitly.
4. Do not weaken assertions, disable checks, manufacture completion data or repair a negative control to make tests pass. A fixture correction requires an independent direct-target baseline and an explanation of the original error; keep the failure evidence.
5. Package tests, race detection, vet and builds are necessary engineering checks. Their totals are not a product-readiness score. No percentages, “all done,” “day one ready” or equivalent claim while a required acceptance row is FAIL or NOT RUN.
6. A documented limitation remains unfinished work when it contradicts this contract. Release notes, a renamed milestone or a smaller demo do not constitute a scope change. Any exception requires an explicit operator scope decision recorded with its impact.
7. Final acceptance requires the evidence-backed matrix and explicit operator sign-off. Commits, pushes and deployments remain separate actions governed by the user's current authorization; passing checks does not authorize replacing a live session.
